About Active Statics

Active Statics is an interactive educational tool designed by Simon Greenwold at MIT Media Lab's Aesthetics + Computation Group in 2001. It helps students and engineers explore the principles of graphic statics and structural analysis through hands-on manipulation.

These applets demonstrate how forces distribute through different structural systems. You can drag nodes, adjust loads, and watch force polygons update in real-time to build intuition about structural behavior.

How to use:

  • Click on any applet below to launch it
  • Look for yellow circles - these are draggable nodes
  • Use toggle buttons to enable/disable various features and constraints
  • Watch how force polygons and member forces change as you manipulate the structure

Note: These original Java applets are running via CheerpJ, which converts Java bytecode to WebAssembly and JavaScript. First load may take a moment while the JVM initializes.

3. Hanging Cable / Arch
Analyze funicular structures - hanging cables in tension or compression arches. See how loads create natural catenary curves.
🌉
4. Cantilever Truss
Study cantilevered structures and understand how overhanging members resolve forces back to the support points.
🎯
5. Fanlike Structure (Cable Stay)
Explore cable-stayed bridges and structures with radiating tension members in a fan-like arrangement.
🏛️
6. Overhanging Truss
Investigate trusses with extended overhanging sections and see how forces redistribute beyond support points.

7. Minimum Weight Truss
Explore structural optimization - trusses designed for minimum weight while maintaining required strength and stability.

8. Beam Loading
